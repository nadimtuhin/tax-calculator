## Investment tax rebate — section 78

For **AY 2027–28** (income year 2026–27), the Finance Act 2026 amended section 78 of the Income Tax Act 2023 so an individual’s investment tax rebate is the **lowest** of:

| Component | AY 2027–28 (Finance Act 2026) | AY 2026–27 (Finance Ordinance 2025) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Percentage of taxable income | **3%** of total taxable income, excluding exempt income, reduced-rate/final-tax income, and partnership/AoP profit share | **3%** on the same basis |
| Percentage of eligible investment | **10%** of qualifying investment/expenses under Part 3, Sixth Schedule | **15%** of qualifying investment/expenses |
| Absolute cap | **Tk 750,000** | **Tk 10,00,000 (Tk 1 million)** |

KPMG’s Finance Bill 2026 summary states the new rebate is the lowest of 3% of total taxable income, 10% of actual eligible investment, and Tk 750,000; its Finance Ordinance 2025 summary confirms the prior AY 2026–27 rule was 3%, 15%, and Tk 1 million.  PwC’s updated individual tax calculation likewise applies 3% of total income, 10% of allowable investment, and a Tk 750,000 ceiling, taking the lowest amount.  The NBR lists Finance Act 2026 as enacted on 6 July 2026.[1][2][3][4]

**Example:** If taxable income is Tk 20 lakh and eligible investment is Tk 6 lakh, the three amounts are Tk 60,000 (3%), Tk 60,000 (10%), and Tk 750,000; the rebate is **Tk 60,000**. Under the prior AY 2026–27 rule, 15% of Tk 6 lakh would have been Tk 90,000, so the rebate could have been higher if income-based 3% did not bind.

## Salary exemption — Sixth Schedule

The salary-income exemption remains the **lower of one-third of salary or Tk 5,00,000** for AY 2027–28. The Finance Act 2026 raised the monetary ceiling from **Tk 4,50,000 to Tk 5,00,000**; the one-third-of-salary test is unchanged.[1][5]

So, for a salaried individual:
- Salary up to Tk 15 lakh: exempt amount is one-third of salary.
- Salary above Tk 15 lakh: exempt amount is capped at **Tk 5 lakh**.

This is an increase of Tk 50,000 in the maximum salary exemption compared with AY 2026–27, when the cap was Tk 4.5 lakh.  PwC’s current Bangladesh individual-tax material reflects the post-Finance Act 2026 framework, including the revised rebate parameters.[3][5]
