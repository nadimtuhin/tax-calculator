## Bottom line

- **AY 2027-28:** the tax-free threshold for gazetted war-wounded freedom fighters and July Warriors is **Tk 550,000**, not Tk 575,000.[1][2]
- **AY 2026-27:** the Finance Ordinance 2025 originally set it at **Tk 525,000**, but the later Finance Act 2026 revised the AY 2026-27 and 2027-28 threshold upward to **Tk 550,000**. So the *final* figure is Tk 550,000; Tk 525,000 was only the Ordinance-stage figure.[1][2][3]
- **Disabled and third-gender taxpayers, AY 2026-27:** likewise, Finance Ordinance 2025 set **Tk 500,000**, but Finance Act 2026 raised the final AY 2026-27/2027-28 threshold to **Tk 525,000**.[1][2][3]
- **Salary exemption cap:** the original cap was **Tk 450,000**; it was raised to **Tk 500,000 by Finance Ordinance 2025**, applicable from AY 2026-27—not by Finance Act 2026.[4][5]

## Thresholds: exact sources

KPMG’s *Salient features of Finance Ordinance 2025* states, for proposed AY 2026-27 and 2027-28:

> “Gazetted war-wounded freedom fighters and July Warriors — Taka 525,000”; “Third gender — Taka 500,000”; “Physically challenged persons — Taka 500,000.”[3]

KPMG’s *Salient features of Finance Bill 2026* then shows the revised schedule:

> “Gazetted war-wounded freedom fighters and July Warriors — Taka 525,000” for AY 2026-27/2027-28 in the Bill, rising to “Taka 550,000” for AY 2028-29/2029-30.[2]

However, the enacted Finance Act 2026 practitioner summary (Tuhin & Partners) reports the final Schedule 1 position as:

> “Gazetted freedom fighters; July fighters — 5,50,000” for **AY 2026-27 & 2027-28**; “Third gender; persons with disabilities — 5,25,000” for the same years.[1]

ACNABIN’s Finance Act 2026 booklet likewise presents the enacted AY 2026-27/2027-28 general threshold as Tk 400,000 and confirms that Finance Act 2026 changed the individual tax-free levels for those assessment years.[6]

### Reconciling the conflict

| Category | Finance Ordinance 2025 (AY 2026-27 & 2027-28) | Finance Act 2026, final (AY 2026-27 & 2027-28) | Correct final answer |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Gazetted war-wounded freedom fighters / July Warriors | Tk 525,000 [3] | Tk 550,000 [1] | **Tk 550,000** |
| Persons with disabilities | Tk 500,000 [3] | Tk 525,000 [1] | **Tk 525,000** |
| Third-gender taxpayers | Tk 500,000 [3] | Tk 525,000 [1] | **Tk 525,000** |

The apparent “Tk 575,000” figure belongs to the **later** schedule: KPMG’s Finance Bill 2026 table lists Tk 575,000 for third-gender and physically challenged taxpayers in **AY 2028-29 and 2029-30**, and PwC’s current summary likewise lists Tk 575,000 for those categories and Tk 600,000 for freedom fighters/July fighters in the FY 2027/28–2028/29 presentation—i.e., the following two-year block, not AY 2027-28.[2][7]

## Salary-income exemption cap

Under the Income Tax Act 2023, employment income includes an exemption equal to the **lower of one-third of salary income or a monetary ceiling**. The ceiling was originally **Tk 450,000**.[5]

ASICO’s summary of Finance Ordinance 2025 states directly:

> “Para 27: Maximum exemption on salary income is increased from Tk. 450,000 to Tk. 500,000.”[4]

TaxpertBD gives the same rule and timing: “a salaried person deducts 1/3 of total salary income or BDT 450,000; whichever is lower,” and “Finance Ordinance 2025 extended this the maximum ceiling to BDT 500,000.”[5]

Therefore: **Tk 450,000 was the original cap; Finance Ordinance 2025 raised it to Tk 500,000 for AY 2026-27 onward. Finance Act 2026 did not make that increase.**[4][5]

## Practical filing rule

For AY 2026-27 and AY 2027-28, use the **enacted Finance Act 2026** figures: Tk 550,000 for gazetted war-wounded freedom fighters and July Warriors, and Tk 525,000 for disabled and third-gender taxpayers. The Finance Ordinance 2025 figures of Tk 525,000 and Tk 500,000 are historically correct but superseded for those assessment years.[1][2][3]
